# Trend analysis: what similar brands, platforms and creators are talking about

Prepared 22 November 2026 for Simply Being Female. Based on public web sources (listed at the end). Most trend writing is from vendor blogs and magazines, so treat percentages as directional and check the original report before quoting any figure in SBF content (brand rule: never invent or loosely repeat statistics).

## The short version

1. **Small, warm communities are beating big reach.** This is the single most repeated finding across 2026 trend reports, and it is exactly SBF's model (circles, the Sunday note, coaching).
2. **The conversation women are having right now is money, menopause at work and friendship.** All three map directly to SBF's audience (28 to 55, at a turning point).
3. **The dates that matter in the next three weeks:** 16 Days of Activism (25 Nov to 10 Dec), the tail end of Equal Pay Day coverage (UK 20 Nov, France 19 Nov), then end-of-year reflection content.
4. **Formats:** carousels on Instagram and LinkedIn, searchable captions, replying to every comment within a day, and longer partnerships with a few creators rather than one-off posts.

## 1. What women are talking about

### Money and the pay gap
- Equal Pay Day landed early this year in the UK because the gap widened. France marked its own "unpaid" day on 19 November. Coverage centres on salary transparency and the "ambition penalty": women who push for more get pushed back.
- **SBF angle (fits "Say it out loud" and "I stopped leaving honey on the table"):** the conversation you rehearse four times before asking for a raise. Practical scripts, not outrage.

### Menopause and careers
- Mid-November news coverage reports women taking pay cuts or stepping back at work while managing menopause, often at the peak of their careers (director and VP level).
- Creators who began as clinicians or journalists have built whole businesses on menopause education. The space is crowded with medical advice; it is thin on community and honest storytelling.
- **SBF angle:** not medical advice (leave that to doctors) but the room where you can say it out loud. "Nothing on menopause yet. Rude, we know." is already an approved-style line. A circle or podcast episode on menopause at work would sit in a clear gap.

### Friendship and loneliness in midlife
- Writers are calling it the "friendship recession": fewer casual contacts, fewer friends after 40 and 50. A counter-trend says midlife women are choosing smaller, closer circles on purpose.
- Women-only friendship communities are growing (one UK club reports over 100,000 members, self-reported).
- New worry: AI companions filling the gap.
- **SBF angle (fits "We saved you a seat" and "Women who've got your back"):** this is the strongest fit of all. SBF circles are the answer to the friendship recession. Lead with the specific detail: the woman who has a group chat for everything except how she actually feels.

### Wellbeing and social media pressure (Australia)
- Beyond Blue's 2026 check: women more likely than men to report mental-health symptoms, and concern about social media rose (27% to 33%).
- The 2026 World Happiness Report links heavy social media use to falling wellbeing, especially among teenage girls. Australia ranked 15th.
- Wellness commentary describes "social wellness" (group activities) replacing solo self-care.
- **SBF angle:** in-person circles as the antidote to the scroll. Careful, honest tone: no promises of cures.

## 3. What is working on the platforms

- **Community over reach.** Micro-communities with recurring series, inside jokes and identity markers outperform broad reach. A recurring weekly series (for example a Sunday note preview every Friday) helps.
- **Reply fast.** Roughly three in four social users expect a reply within 24 hours. The studio's Community inbox already helps here.
- **Carousels.** On Instagram they earn more engagement per person reached than Reels (Reels reach more people). On LinkedIn, document carousels are the top format by a wide margin in Buffer's 45M-post study.
- **Social search.** Nearly one in three people start searches on TikTok, Instagram or YouTube. Captions, on-screen text and alt text should use the words women actually search ("menopause at work", "making friends after 40", "how to ask for a raise").
- **Longer video is back.** Reels up to 20 minutes and 10-minute TikTok uploads: room for circle highlights and podcast clips.
- **LinkedIn** rewards personal, confessional posts over corporate ones. That suits the "confess first" founder habit.
- **Creator partnerships** are moving from one-off posts to longer relationships based on audience fit.

## 4. Suggested content for the next three weeks

All drafts should go through the studio's normal approval flow and brand check.

1. **16 Days of Activism (25 Nov to 10 Dec).** The 2026 UN theme is "Ensure justice, protect survivors, end impunity"; UN Women Australia uses "Safe. Everywhere. Always." Keep SBF's tone friendship-focused and practical: where to get help (1800RESPECT), how to be the friend who notices. Avoid combative language. Consider one considered post rather than a daily series; commentators are already criticising brands that treat it as a ritual rather than action.
2. **The raise conversation** carousel: five honest scripts, ending "Get the Sunday note".
3. **Menopause at work** circle or podcast episode, with a qualified guest. Headline idea: "Nobody put it on the agenda. *So we did*."
4. **Friendship recession** founder post: confess first. Ends with a question: "When did you last make a new friend on purpose?"
5. **End-of-year reflection** (early December): "The letter you'd write if you weren't being polite", addressed to 2026.
